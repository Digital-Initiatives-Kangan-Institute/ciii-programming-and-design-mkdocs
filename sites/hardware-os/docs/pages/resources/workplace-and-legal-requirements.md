# Workplace and Legal Requirements

Technology decisions in a workplace are never purely technical. Organisations set their own rules about how technology is used, and legislation sets wider legal boundaries. Both apply when installing, configuring, or using ICT equipment and software.

---

## Rules Set by the Organisation

Organisations document their expectations so that systems stay secure, consistent, supportable, and lawful. These documents go by names such as policies, procedures, standards, and guidelines, and the details vary from one workplace to the next.

Most organisations have rules covering similar ground. Some of the common areas include:

- **Installing and changing software** — who may install software, what needs approval beforehand, and how changes are documented and tested before going live
- **Protecting information and systems** — requirements for passwords, user accounts, encryption, and the handling of sensitive data
- **Acceptable use** — what staff may and may not do with computers, networks, and internet access
- **Backups and recovery** — what gets backed up, how often, where copies are kept, and how data is restored
- **Procurement and purchasing** — which products are approved and how new hardware and software are obtained
- **Asset and access management** — tracking who has which equipment and controlling who can access systems

The exact names and content differ between organisations, but the underlying ideas are similar: get approval first, protect data and systems, document what was done, and keep everything consistent.

When using a new technology, the first step is to check which policies and procedures apply and follow them. If something is unclear, the safe approach is to ask a supervisor rather than assume.

---

## Rules Set by Legislation

Alongside internal rules, Australian law applies to the use of ICT technology. The relevant areas include:

- **Privacy** — the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles regulate how organisations collect, use, store, and disclose personal information
- **Intellectual property and copyright** — the Copyright Act 1968 protects software, documents, images, and other digital content, meaning software must be used legally and in line with its licence
- **Electronic messaging** — the Spam Act 2003 restricts the sending of unsolicited commercial electronic messages
- **Workplace health and safety** — WHS legislation requires workplaces to manage risks from equipment, including workstations, cabling, and electrical safety
- **Software licensing** — licences set the terms under which software may be used, such as the number of installations, permitted users, and whether commercial use is allowed
- **Data breach notification** — the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme requires organisations to report certain serious data breaches

Some organisations also operate in regulated industries with additional rules of their own. The key point is that using ICT technology responsibly means checking both the organisation's internal policies and the relevant external legal requirements.

---

## Summary

- Organisations set policies and procedures covering installation, security, acceptable use, backups, procurement, and access
- Legislation such as privacy, copyright, spam, and workplace health and safety laws also applies to ICT use
- Software licences add contractual limits on how software may be used
- Before using new technology, check both organisational rules and legal requirements
