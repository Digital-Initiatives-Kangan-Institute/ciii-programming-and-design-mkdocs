# Module 4: Visualising Data

## Module Overview

Once data has been imported and organised into a semantic model, it can be transformed into meaningful visualisations that help users identify trends, compare performance, and make informed decisions.

Power BI provides a wide range of visualisation options, each designed to communicate information in different ways. Choosing the correct visual is important because different business questions require different forms of analysis.

In this module, students will learn how to create and format common Power BI visualisations and understand when each visual type should be used.

---

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this module, students will be able to:

- Explain the purpose of data visualisation.
- Select appropriate visualisations for different business questions.
- Create comparison charts.
- Create part-to-whole charts.
- Create tables and matrix visuals.
- Apply basic formatting to improve readability.
- Use visualisations to explore data from a semantic model.

---

## What is Data Visualisation?

Data visualisation is the process of presenting information visually to make it easier to understand.

Visualisations help users:

- Identify trends.
- Compare values.
- Detect patterns.
- Spot anomalies.
- Communicate findings.

Rather than reviewing hundreds of rows of data, users can quickly understand information through charts and graphs.

---

## Principles of Effective Visualisation

Good visualisations communicate information clearly and efficiently.

When creating visuals:

- Choose the correct chart type.
- Keep layouts simple.
- Use clear labels.
- Avoid unnecessary colours.
- Focus on the business question being answered.
- Highlight important insights.

The goal is to help users understand information as quickly as possible.

---

## Understanding Business Questions

Before creating a visual, it is important to understand the question being asked.

### Examples

#### Comparison Questions

- Which category generated the highest sales?
- Which region performed best?
- Which product sold the most units?

Comparison charts are often the most suitable option.

---

#### Trend Questions

- How have sales changed over time?
- Is profit increasing or decreasing?
- Are customer numbers growing?

Line charts are often the most suitable option.

---

#### Composition Questions

- What percentage of sales came from each category?
- How is revenue distributed across products?
- Which stages contribute to customer loss?

Part-to-whole charts are often the most suitable option.

---

## Comparison Charts

Comparison charts are designed to compare values between categories.

---

## Bar Charts

Bar charts are one of the most commonly used visualisations.

They are used to compare values across categories.

### Best Uses

- Sales by category
- Revenue by state
- Orders by customer segment
- Product performance comparisons

### Advantages

- Easy to understand
- Easy to compare values
- Suitable for large category sets

### Example Questions

- Which category generated the most sales?
- Which customer segment placed the most orders?
- Which state has the highest revenue?

---

## Column Charts

Column charts are similar to bar charts but display data vertically.

### Best Uses

- Monthly comparisons
- Quarterly performance
- Yearly summaries

### Advantages

- Familiar format
- Easy to compare time periods

### Example Questions

- Which month had the highest sales?
- How did each quarter perform?

---

## Line Charts

Line charts show how values change over time.

They are ideal for identifying trends and patterns.

### Best Uses

- Sales trends
- Revenue growth
- Customer growth
- Website traffic

### Advantages

- Highlights direction and movement
- Reveals seasonal patterns
- Makes trends easy to identify

### Example Questions

- Are sales increasing?
- When did revenue peak?
- Which periods experienced decline?

---

## Combo Charts

Combo charts combine two visual types into a single chart.

Typically:

- Columns display one measure.
- Lines display another measure.

### Best Uses

- Comparing revenue and profit
- Comparing orders and revenue
- Comparing sales and growth rates

### Advantages

- Allows comparison of multiple measures.
- Saves dashboard space.
- Highlights relationships between metrics.

### Example Questions

- Do higher sales produce higher profit?
- How do order volumes compare to revenue over time?

---

## Part-to-Whole Charts

Part-to-whole charts show how individual categories contribute to a total.

---

## Pie Charts

Pie charts display proportions as slices of a circle.

### Best Uses

- Small numbers of categories
- Percentage breakdowns

### Advantages

- Easy to understand
- Suitable for simple comparisons

### Limitations

- Difficult to compare many categories
- Less effective when category values are similar

### Example Questions

- What percentage of sales comes from each category?
- How is revenue distributed across regions?

---

## Donut Charts

Donut charts are similar to pie charts but contain an empty centre section.

### Best Uses

- Percentage comparisons
- Displaying a KPI in the centre

### Advantages

- Modern appearance
- Useful in dashboards

### Example Questions

- What proportion of revenue comes from each customer segment?
- How are customers distributed across categories?

---

## Funnel Charts

Funnel charts display values across a sequence of stages.

### Best Uses

- Sales pipelines
- Recruitment processes
- Customer journeys
- Marketing campaigns

### Advantages

- Highlights drop-off between stages
- Supports process analysis

### Example Questions

- How many leads became customers?
- At which point are customers lost?

---

## Treemaps

Treemaps use nested rectangles to display proportions.

The size of each rectangle represents its value.

### Best Uses

- Category comparisons
- Hierarchical data
- Large category sets

### Advantages

- Efficient use of space
- Displays many categories simultaneously

### Example Questions

- Which product categories contribute most to revenue?
- Which products dominate sales?

---

## Tables

Tables present data in rows and columns.

They provide detailed information rather than summaries.

### Best Uses

- Detailed reporting
- Transaction review
- Data exploration

### Advantages

- Exact values displayed
- Familiar structure
- Easy to sort and filter

### Example Questions

- Which customers placed orders this month?
- What products were purchased?
- What discounts were applied?

---

## Matrix Visuals

A matrix extends the functionality of a table by supporting grouped information.

### Features

- Expand and collapse levels
- Group categories
- Display subtotals
- Display totals

### Example Uses

- Sales by category and product
- Revenue by state and suburb
- Orders by year and month

### Advantages

- Better support for hierarchical analysis
- Easier summarisation of large datasets

---

## Formatting Visualisations

Formatting helps improve readability and professionalism.

---

### Visual Titles

Every visual should have a meaningful title.

Examples:

- Sales by Category
- Revenue by State
- Monthly Sales Trend

Avoid generic titles such as:

- Chart 1
- Visual 2

---

### Colours

Use colours consistently.

Examples:

- Revenue displayed using one colour
- Profit displayed using another colour

Avoid excessive use of colours that distract from the data.

---

### Labels

Labels should be clear and easy to read.

Examples:

- Currency values formatted correctly
- Percentages displayed as percentages
- Dates displayed consistently

---

### Sorting

Charts should be sorted logically.

Examples:

- Highest sales first
- Earliest dates first
- Alphabetical ordering when appropriate

Sorting often improves readability significantly.

---

## Choosing the Right Visual

| Business Question | Recommended Visual |
|---|---|
| Compare categories | Bar Chart |
| Compare time periods | Column Chart |
| Show a trend | Line Chart |
| Compare multiple measures | Combo Chart |
| Show proportions | Pie or Donut Chart |
| Show process stages | Funnel Chart |
| Show category contribution | Treemap |
| Display detailed records | Table |
| Display grouped information | Matrix |
