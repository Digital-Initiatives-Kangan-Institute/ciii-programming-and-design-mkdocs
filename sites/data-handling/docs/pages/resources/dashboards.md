# Module 7: Dashboards

## Module Overview

Dashboards provide a high-level view of business performance and are often used by managers and executives to monitor key metrics and trends.

A well-designed dashboard brings together important information onto a single page and enables users to understand business performance at a glance.

In this module, students will learn the principles of dashboard design and create a complete dashboard using the semantic model developed throughout this course.

---

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this module, students will be able to:

- Explain the purpose of dashboards.
- Distinguish between reports and dashboards.
- Apply dashboard design principles.
- Create KPI-focused report pages.
- Combine visuals into a meaningful layout.
- Build an executive-style dashboard.

---

## What is a Dashboard?

A dashboard is a collection of visualisations designed to provide a summary of important information.

Dashboards help users:

- Monitor performance
- Identify trends
- Track KPIs
- Support decision-making

---

## Reports vs Dashboards

### Reports

- Detailed analysis
- Multiple pages
- Exploration focused
- Used by analysts

### Dashboards

- Summary information
- Single-page focus
- Monitoring focused
- Used by managers and executives

---

## Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

KPIs are measurable values used to assess performance.

Examples include:

- Total Sales
- Total Orders
- Number of Customers
- Revenue Growth
- Average Order Value

Good dashboards focus on a small number of meaningful KPIs.

---

## Dashboard Design Principles

### Simplicity

Users should understand the dashboard quickly.

Avoid:

- Unnecessary visuals
- Excessive colours
- Information overload

---

### Visual Hierarchy

Place the most important information where it is easy to find.

Typically:

- KPIs at the top
- Key charts in the middle
- Detailed information lower on the page

---

### Consistency

Use:

- Consistent colours
- Consistent formatting
- Consistent naming

This improves usability and readability.

---

### Relevance

Every visual should answer a business question.

Ask:

> What decision will this visual help support?

If the answer is unclear, reconsider including the visual.

---

## Dashboard Components

### KPI Cards

Used to display:

- Total Customers
- Total Products
- Total Sales
- Total Orders

Benefits:

- Easy to understand
- Highly visible
- Useful for executives

---

### Comparison Visuals

Useful for comparing performance across groups.

Examples:

- Sales by Category
- Sales by State

---

### Trend Visuals

Useful for displaying:

- Monthly sales trends
- Growth patterns
- Seasonal behaviour

---

### Composition Visuals

Useful for displaying:

- Customer segments
- Product categories
- State distribution

---

## Designing for the Audience

Different users require different information.

### Executives

Focus on:

- KPIs
- Trends
- High-level summaries

---

### Managers

Focus on:

- Team performance
- Operational insights
- Category performance

---

### Analysts

Focus on:

- Detailed exploration
- Supporting data
- Investigation tools

## Dashboard Best Practices

- Keep dashboards to a single page where possible.
- Prioritise important information.
- Use clear titles.
- Remove unnecessary clutter.
- Use whitespace effectively.
- Test interactivity before sharing.
- Ensure visuals support business goals.
